# OrbitWatch: Mission Carbon

**Explore Earth. Build your satellite. Investigate our atmosphere.**

## About the Project

OrbitWatch: Mission Carbon is an interactive educational game I developed to help people understand how satellites observe Earth and how the information they collect can be used to study our environment.

I wanted to create something that makes learning about atmospheric carbon dioxide and environmental conditions more interesting. Instead of just reading about satellites and scientific measurements, players can experience a simplified satellite mission themselves.

The game combines simulated missions with environmental data from NASA, allowing players to explore different regions, make decisions, collect observations, and analyze their results.

## How the Game Works

Players begin by preparing their own satellite mission. They choose an environment to investigate, customize their satellite, and enter Mission Control.

During the mission, they must manage their satellite's energy and resources while collecting and transmitting observations.

After gathering enough information, players can investigate their measurements, answer scientific questions, and review their mission results.

## Main Features

### Satellite Builder

Players can customize their satellite and prepare it for an Earth observation mission.

### Mission Target Selection

Players can explore different environments, including forests, urban areas, industrial regions, and polar regions, before choosing where to conduct their mission.

### Mission Control

Players manage their satellite using several actions:

- Scan regions to collect observations.
- Transmit collected measurements.
- Conserve energy.
- Adjust the satellite's orbit.

These actions affect the progress of the mission and encourage players to think about how they use their available resources.

### Data Investigation Lab

Players can examine the CO₂ measurements collected during their missions through interactive graphs and scientific activities.

They can record observations, identify patterns, calculate results, and write conclusions based on the information they collected.

### NASA Environmental Data

The game incorporates NASA environmental information, including air temperature and humidity, to help players understand the conditions of different environments.

### Mission Reports

At the end of a mission, players can review their results and reflect on the observations they collected.

## Scientific Data and Simulations

OrbitWatch uses both real environmental data and simulated satellite measurements.

The CO₂ measurements generated during gameplay are simulated for educational purposes. They allow players to practice collecting, interpreting, and analyzing scientific observations.

The project also uses NASA POWER environmental data and includes functionality related to NASA's Orbiting Carbon Observatory-2 (OCO-2), which studies atmospheric carbon dioxide.

Simulated measurements are distinguished from real scientific data within the project.

## How to Run the Game

To run OrbitWatch locally, you will need Python installed on your computer.

1. Download or clone this repository.
2. Open the project folder in a terminal.
3. Install the required libraries:

   ```bash
   pip install -r requirements.txt
   ```

4. Start the application:

   ```bash
   py -m streamlit run app.py
   ```

5. The game should open in your web browser.

## Why I Created OrbitWatch

I've always been interested in programming and wanted to create a project that combined technology with something meaningful.

I chose to focus on satellites and Earth's atmosphere because satellite observations play an important role in understanding our planet, but the science behind them can sometimes be difficult to understand.

With OrbitWatch, I wanted to turn some of those ideas into an interactive experience where players could learn by experimenting, making decisions, and analyzing data.

Working on this project also helped me learn more about developing interactive applications, working with scientific information, and solving programming problems.
